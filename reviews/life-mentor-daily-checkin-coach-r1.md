# Review r1: Life Mentor / Daily Check-in Coach

## Overall score

**6 / 10**

Not ready to publish. A publishable template scores 9 or higher. This draft already has the right shape: four life areas, a one-time setup, a daily check-in, a weekly review, and written bans on medical, legal, and financial advice and on spending or sending. Those bans do not survive contact with the skills. A stranger who imports the file as written can send a weekly life summary to someone else, coach "income growth," treat another bot as a professional, and get no usable crisis path. Schedules and question limits also contradict each other, so the first week will not match the storefront promise.

The packed file reviewed here is `templates/life-mentor-daily-checkin-coach.md` after the internal status line was removed. The template body was not rewritten.

## (a) Personal-data or identity leaks

No real person, town, email, phone, account number, company, client, or URL remains after the status line was removed. Two setup fingerprints still look like the author's private bot fleet.

1. **Named companion bots in getting-started.** Question 5 tells the new owner to hand topics to "a health-appointments bot or an admin/finance bot." Those read as real bot names from a private set, not as generic examples. **Suggested fix:** Delete both names. Ask only: "If you already use another assistant for scheduling or paperwork, what should I call it? If not, I will point you to a professional when a topic is outside habits."

2. **Work-bot / home-bot split.** The privacy fact says that if the owner has separate work and home bots, the coach should "use only summaries across that line and never copy work data into home memory." That documents a private two-bot layout and still allows work content, including employer or client details, to be rewritten into this bot as a summary. **Suggested fix:** Delete the sentence. Replace it with: "Do not read or write any other bot's memory. Discuss only what the owner says in this chat."

## (b) Safety gaps

3. **Weekly summary can be sent to someone else without a yes for that send.** Getting-started question 6 asks "Who, if anyone, should get a short weekly summary?" and the weekly routine then "Send[s] the summary only to the recipient chosen during getting-started" on every review day. One setup answer is not an explicit yes for each specific send. The safety fact forbids sending email or DMs without that yes. The summary covers health, family, and money. The question also invites storing another person's name or contact, which the privacy fact forbids. No channel is named, so the model may email or message a third party. **Suggested fix:** Deliver the weekly review only in this chat, to the owner. Remove question 6 and the "summary recipient" memory. If a share feature stays, require all of the following each time: the owner names the channel, the owner says what may be included, and the owner says yes to that specific send. Never ask for or store another person's email, phone, or handle.

4. **Family coaching collects other people's lives.** The job fact says the family area is "how the people who matter to the owner are doing." The privacy fact says not to store other people's sensitive details. Skill 1 says family goals must be the owner's own actions and must not target other people's bodies, health, or behaviour. A model will follow the job line and start a record of partners, children, or relatives. **Suggested fix:** Change the job fact to match skill 1: family goals are only actions the owner controls (time, plans, errands). Do not ask how other people feel, and do not write notes about them. Keep the example goal about the owner's own time, and drop any prompt that asks for a status report on a named person.

5. **Money area gives financial advice.** The storefront label and the boundaries fact promise no financial advice. The job fact still offers "ideas and planning for income growth and spending habits." Income-growth ideas are advice (raises, side work, products), not a habit log. Skill 1 is narrower ("review spending every Sunday," never specific investments) and will lose to the job fact. **Suggested fix:** Make the job fact match skill 1 and the label. Money help is limited to habits the owner names, such as a weekly spending review. Do not suggest ways to earn more, products, accounts, or investments. Do not comment on whether a purchase, debt, or job move is wise.

6. **Another bot is treated as a qualified professional.** Boundaries say that for symptoms, medication, mental health, legal, tax, or investment questions, the coach should suggest a qualified professional "or the owner's matching specialist bot." Question 5 then offers to "hand topics to" that bot. A specialist bot is not a clinician, lawyer, or adviser, and forwarding the owner's words is a disclosure. **Suggested fix:** For those topics, tell the owner to talk to a qualified human professional, and keep coaching only the habit side if they still want that. Do not describe another bot as the safety path. Never forward a message. If the owner wants a note passed to a bot they named, ask for a yes for that note, and do not include symptoms, diagnoses, money details, account data, or information about other people.

7. **Crisis handling will not hold.** The boundaries fact says to stop coaching and urge "local emergency services or a crisis line" if the owner mentions a crisis, self-harm, or danger to anyone. It does not name a service, does not say to stop the daily and weekly routines, does not forbid saving the incident into goals or progress notes, and does not say what to do if the owner may be a minor. The next scheduled check-in would resume habits as if nothing happened. "A crisis line" is not something a person in distress can use without a name. **Suggested fix:** Add a short crisis rule and use it instead of any skill: stop questions, do not store the details, pause both routines until the owner explicitly asks to resume coaching, and tell them to contact local emergency services now. If they are in the United States or Canada, tell them they can call or text 988. Otherwise tell them to use their local crisis line. If they might be under 18, also tell them to contact a trusted adult. Do not keep coaching in that conversation.

8. **Abuse or danger to a child is coached as a habit.** Skill 1's family example is "one-on-one time with each kid weekly." Nothing says to leave that script when the owner describes abuse, violence, or a child or other person in danger. The crisis line covers "danger to anyone" only if the model notices it; the family skill does not point there. **Suggested fix:** If the owner describes abuse, violence, or someone else in danger, including a child, leave goal-setting and follow the crisis rule in flag 7. Do not propose contact, confrontation, or time-with-a-child goals in that situation.

9. **Health scope and the spoken disclaimer are wider than the promise.** Health habits include "energy, stress, and keeping appointments," which pulls the coach into mood, symptoms, and medical scheduling. "Book" is banned only in the safety fact, not in the health skill. The greeting says "I don't give medical, legal or financial advice" and drops the boundaries fact's psychological, tax, and investment bans, so the owner hears a shorter promise than the memory stores. **Suggested fix:** Limit health goals to sleep timing and movement the owner chooses. No symptom interpretation, medication, diagnosis, or stress counseling. Appointment help, if the owner asks, is a reminder with no clinic name, clinician, or reason, and any booking still needs a separate explicit yes. Make the greeting list the same bans as the boundaries fact: medical, psychological, legal, tax, and investment.

10. **Silence does not stop the daily messages.** Skill 2 says that after three missed check-ins the bot sends one "still want daily check-ins, or switch to weekly?" message and then respects the answer. The daily routine still runs every day unless "the owner has paused check-ins," and nothing defines pause, what a non-answer means, or how to switch to weekly. The bot can nudge and keep showing up. **Suggested fix:** After three misses, send that one question and pause the daily routine immediately. No reply means stay paused. In the getting-started close, tell the owner the controls in plain language: "pause check-ins," "resume daily," "switch to weekly," "change my time," and "reset goals."

11. **Quiet hours can lose to the chosen clock time.** Quiet hours are "{your quiet hours} ... unless the owner asks otherwise." Getting-started says to set both routines "outside quiet hours." Both routine schedules repeat "outside quiet hours" and also "at the time chosen." There is no winner when the owner picks 7:00 and quiet hours run until 7:30, and no rule for a one-off "message me anyway." **Suggested fix:** State one rule: do not message during quiet hours. If a proposed check-in or review time falls inside them, do not save it; ask for a time outside them. The only override is a specific yes that names the routine and the new time.

12. **Progress notes can become a health record.** Privacy allows "streaks and short progress notes" and forbids medical records and diagnoses. Daily check-in logs "done/not done" and also "Log the answers," and the health area invites sleep, stress, energy, and appointment content. Those answers are health data. **Suggested fix:** Log only the habit result the owner agreed to track, such as "walk: done" or "walk: not done." Do not store symptoms, sleep quality, mood, doses, diagnoses, clinic names, or reasons for appointments.

13. **Resetting goals can delete the saved list without a specific yes.** Skill 1 says "Save the list." The weekly review "offer[s] to run goal-setting again" at quarter end, and getting-started says the owner can reset later only by implication. Replacing the list is a delete. The safety fact requires an explicit yes for that specific delete. **Suggested fix:** Before replacing or discarding saved goals, show the current list and ask "Replace these goals?" Wait for a yes that refers to that list. A new quarter, or running the skill again, does not count as that yes.

## (c) Clarity and usability for a stranger

14. **"Template draft" is still the title.** The H1 is "Template draft: Life Mentor / Daily Check-in Coach." The internal status line is gone; the packing label is not. An importer will think the file is unfinished or private. **Suggested fix:** Use the public profile name alone as the H1.

15. **Brace placeholders ship in memory.** The quiet-hours fact is `{your quiet hours}` in `{your timezone}`, and getting-started says to replace those tokens later. Nothing tells a stranger whether the braces are platform variables or literal text. Until setup finishes, the bot can recite the braces, and a platform that does not treat them as variables will store them as the owner's hours. **Suggested fix:** Ship the fact as "Quiet hours: not set. Timezone: not set. Ask in getting-started before any routine runs." Delete the brace tokens from the profile and from the setup instructions.

16. **The weekly routine needs a time that setup never asks for.** Question 3 asks "Which day works for a 5-minute weekly review?" Question 2 asks for a daily time only. The weekly schedule still says "weekly on the day and time chosen during getting-started," and setup says "Set the two routines to the chosen times." There is no second time to save. **Suggested fix:** Ask "What time on that day should I send the weekly review?" and save it. If you want fewer questions, state in question 3, the log line, and the weekly schedule that the review uses the daily check-in time on the chosen day.

17. **Routines are not importable, and they can fire before setup.** Both schedules are prose ("daily at the time chosen during getting-started"). Plugins are "None." No chat or channel is named. Nothing says to stay silent when timezone, check-in time, or review day is still empty, so a scheduler can run a check-in before getting-started, or not run at all. **Suggested fix:** On both routines, add: "If the check-in time, review day, or timezone is not saved yet, do not message; run getting-started instead." State that delivery is this chat with the owner only. Replace the prose clock with the platform's schedule field, or add one import note: "After import, set the clock in the routine editor; getting-started will tell the owner which time was saved."

18. **The evening check-in breaks the two-question cap.** Rhythm says "one or two concrete questions at a time." Skill 2 and the daily routine say "Two questions at most." The evening script then asks what got done, what got in the way, and one adjustment for tomorrow: three questions, and it will not stay "under a minute to answer." **Suggested fix:** Make the evening script two questions: "What got done today?" and "What is the one change for tomorrow?" Keep the same cap in the rhythm fact, skill 2, and the daily routine.

19. **Quarterly goal count disagrees with itself.** Structure says "at most 1–2 per area." Skill 1 says "one measurable quarterly goal." **Suggested fix:** Pick a single cap and use it in both places. One goal per area matches the "small steps" promise; write "one quarterly goal per area" in the structure fact and in skill 1.

20. **"90 days" and "quarter end" are different clocks.** Skill 1 asks what "better in 90 days" looks like and runs "at each quarter start." Skill 3 compares results "at quarter end" and "at the end of a quarter." A goal saved on 10 May does not end on a calendar quarter boundary, and the template never says which clock wins. **Suggested fix:** Define one cycle. Either goals last 90 days from the day they are saved, and the review offers a reset when those 90 days end, or goals follow calendar quarters, and skill 1 drops "90 days." Use that same phrase in skill 1, skill 3, and the weekly routine.

21. **Area names change between sections.** The storefront label says "health" and "money." The job fact and question 4 say "health habits" and "money habits." Skill 1 says "the four areas" without listing them. An owner who opted into "money habits" can still be coached on "income growth" from the job fact. **Suggested fix:** Use one set of labels everywhere: Health habits, Career and learning, Family and relationships, Money habits. Repeat that list in the label, the job fact, skill 1, and question 4.

22. **Profile vs log is unexplained.** The heading says "kind: profile unless marked log," and one bullet is marked log, but a stranger is not told which kind the importer edits, which kind setup may overwrite, or whether log text is shown to the owner. **Suggested fix:** Under the heading, add two sentences: "Profile facts are standing rules. Log facts start empty and are filled during getting-started; the owner may ask to change them later."

## (d) Storefront appeal

23. **The name reads like an internal path.** "Life Mentor / Daily Check-in Coach" joins two products with a slash. It is hard to remember, easy to mis-search, and it looks like a folder label rather than a bot someone would install. The spoken greeting ("I'm your life mentor") and the routine name ("Daily check-in") already split the difference. **Suggested fix:** Publish under one name, "Daily Check-in Coach." Keep "life mentor" only in the greeting if you still want that tone. Use the same name in the H1, the profile name, and the storefront.

24. **The description undersells the product and overclaims safety.** The label is "warm, direct," then four areas, then a daily check-in. It never mentions the five-minute weekly review, which is half the routine design. The second sentence is only a disclaimer. It also says the bot "never spends or sends anything for you," which flag 3 contradicts, so the shelf promise is false. **Suggested fix:** Lead with the cadence and the outcome, then one short boundary. For example: "A one-minute daily check-in and a five-minute weekly review that keep small goals moving in health, work, relationships, and money habits. Planning and habits only; no medical, legal, or financial advice, and it never spends, posts, or messages anyone but you." Do not ship that sentence until the weekly routine actually stays in this chat with the owner.
