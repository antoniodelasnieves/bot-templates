## 1. Strategy

### Channel roles

X (@antoniostoner) is the serial channel. One X Article per bot, released on its own day, plus a launch thread and a small number of single tweets that carry one rule from a template. Readers who care about bots, promises, habits, or family trips should be able to enter on any article and still get the safety rule and the link for that bot. The launch thread is the index. The first reply under it holds the four links and is edited as each article goes live.

LinkedIn (linkedin.com/in/antoniodelasnieves) is the bundle. One post, with an optional carousel, goes up only after all four links are real. The tone is a senior solutions engineer showing four narrow tools, including the ones that touch family logistics. It is a single artifact a peer can forward. It is not a paste of an X thread.

The two channels do different jobs, so they do not carry the same wording on the same day. X teaches one bot. LinkedIn shows the set and the operating rule that runs through it.

### Sequencing and cadence

All times below are local Europe/Madrid. Keep Madrid time as the source of truth.

Clock overlap with the US, while Europe is on CEST (UTC+2) and the US is on daylight time:

- 08:30 Madrid = 02:30 US Eastern = 23:30 US Pacific the evening before. This is an EU and UK morning slot. It is a weak US slot.
- 13:00 Madrid = 07:00 US Eastern = 04:00 US Pacific. Spanish lunch, early US East, US West still quiet.
- 14:30 Madrid = 08:30 US Eastern = 05:30 US Pacific. Best LinkedIn overlap: Spanish work afternoon and US East morning.
- 18:00 Madrid = 12:00 US Eastern = 09:00 US Pacific. Best X overlap: end of the Spanish work day, US East lunch, US West start of the work day.

After the last Sunday of October, Madrid returns to CET (UTC+1). Until US daylight time ends, the gap to the US East shrinks by one hour. Recompute the US columns if the arc slips past that Sunday. Do not move the Madrid times to chase a US clock.

Start on the first Tuesday on which the share links you need for that week's posts are real. If that is the week of 28 September 2026, Week 1 Tuesday is 29 September 2026. The arc from that Tuesday through the recap on the Monday 13 days later is two weeks.

Publish in this order: health check, promise tracker, habit check-in, getaway finder.

The health check goes first because it is the desk the other assistants sit on, and it teaches the needs-you rule in the clearest form. The promise tracker goes second, on a Friday, because the line people can repeat ("OK, on it" is not done) is the one most likely to travel beyond people who already build bots, and Friday is when open loops are visible. The habit check-in goes on a Monday, when a weekly focus is a natural action. The getaway finder goes on a Thursday, ahead of weekend planning, after the earlier posts have already shown that these bots stop before money and messages. LinkedIn closes the set on Friday once all four links exist.

Cadence rules:

- One primary post per release slot. The 18:00 reshare of a morning article is the US door to that same article, not a second topic.
- Empty days stay empty except the two planned standalone rule tweets (Thursday of week 1, Tuesday of week 2). Silence is what makes the articles readable.
- Post the article only when its link works. A missed Wednesday slides the health check to Thursday 08:30 and Thursday's rule tweet drops. Do not stack two articles on one day to "catch up." Shift the later pieces by the same gap, and keep LinkedIn after the fourth link.

### What to measure

For each X post, record a row at 24 hours and again at 72 hours:

- Impressions
- Bookmarks
- Link clicks, once the real URL is in the post
- Replies that ask how a bot behaves, describe a setup, or correct a claim
- Reposts and quote posts
- Follows and profile visits from that post, if the analytics split them
- Article reads, for the four articles, if X shows them

Scoreboard columns: date, Madrid time, piece, impressions, bookmarks, link clicks, question replies, reposts, follows.

Read the scoreboard in that order of importance. Bookmarks and link clicks mean someone may import a template. A question reply tells you which sentence was unclear. Impressions are context, not the goal. Likes are not a row.

LinkedIn, same 24-hour and 72-hour marks: impressions, comments that discuss a real workflow, link clicks, and carousel slide-through if the product shows it. Profile views are secondary.

Decisions, made once:

- After the health-check pair (Wednesday 08:30 vs Wednesday 18:00), compare link clicks and bookmarks. If 18:00 wins, keep long articles in the morning for EU readers and keep the live conversation at 18:00. If 08:30 wins on clicks, move later article publishes to 08:30 and keep a shorter 18:00 pointer. One comparison. Then lock the pattern for the remaining articles.
- After the recap, rank the four links by clicks. That ranking chooses what you document next. It does not change what you claim about these four.
- Quote posts: note whether the quoted line is a rule from a template or a reaction with no content. Rules that get quoted are the lines to reuse. Reactions with no content are not a reason to post more often.

### Reply and engagement

The useful window is the first 75 minutes after an 18:00 post, plus a 15-minute sweep the next morning at 08:15. US West late evening still lands in the Spanish early morning, so the sweep matters.

Reply to questions about behavior, setup, and safety first. Answer with the specific rule from that template, in two or three sentences, and point at the article only when the answer needs the full setup. When someone describes their own roster or an open loop, ask one follow-up that you can actually use ("which two bots both claim that job?"), then stop.

Correct a factual miss once, in public, without a second round. The misses to expect: that the health check edits other bots, that the promise tracker messages teammates, that the habit bot coaches health or money, that the getaway finder books or finds discounts. The corrections are already in the articles.

If someone pastes another person's details, or their own tokens, into a public reply, do not amplify it. Say that setup happens inside their chat, that the health check replaces personal details with generic labels in its reports, and that they should remove secrets from the public reply.

The link stays in the post. Access to a template is not a prize for replying, following, or reposting.

Practices that violate X manipulation rules, and that this launch does not use:

- Asking for likes, reposts, follows, or replies in exchange for the link or for a DM
- "Comment YES", "tag a friend", "like if you agree", or a poll whose only purpose is to inflate a count
- Numbered "reply 1 to 4" mechanics on the launch thread

A person who asks "which one should I import?" gets a direct answer based on the lane they described.

### Repurposing

Each bot produces four surfaces, in this order: the pointer tweet, the X Article, one standalone rule tweet, and one slide in the LinkedIn carousel. The article is the source. The shorter pieces quote a rule that already appears there. They do not add a result.

The launch thread is the only post that presents all four before LinkedIn. The Friday 18:00 X post in week 2 is a link index, not a new essay. The closing Monday recap reuses the questions people actually asked. If a question did not come up, it does not go in the recap.

LinkedIn is written from the four articles after they are frozen. It uses the professional frame (handoffs, acceptance bars, a hard stop before send and spend) and mentions family logistics as part of the same operating problem. It does not reuse tweet cadence, hook numbering, or "thread" language.

Later, optional and outside this two-week arc: one Spanish post per bot, translating claims that are already in the English article, with no new numbers. Run Spanish on a different day from that bot's English article so one release is not split across two audiences in the same news cycle. Do not start the Spanish series until the English recap is done.

Build-in-public material that is allowed: a sentence you had to correct, a setup question people stumbled on, a change you made to a template afterward. Build-in-public material that is not allowed: invented before-and-after metrics, a count of assistants, a discount, or a screenshot that shows anyone else's data.

### Claims you can stand behind

Say this:

- Four free Public Grok Bot templates, named as in the templates.
- You run many personal assistants. You are a senior solutions engineer and a father of three in southern Spain.
- Each bot's actual lane, setup questions, defaults, and stop rules, as written in section 3.
- Family Getaway Finder never promises discounts. Good value means the lowest total price among stays that meet the budget, the must-haves, and the cancellation rule.
- Family Getaway Finder never books, reserves, holds, or pays, even if the owner says yes. Pay-later rates, deposits, no-show fees, and points or credit redemptions count as spending and are never completed by the bot.

Do not say this:

- A specific number of agents, hours saved, money saved, or people using a template
- That any of these bots messages teammates, hosts, or family on its own
- That the habit bot sets health targets or gives financial advice
- That the getaway finder finds deals in the sense of discounts, secret rates, or cheaper-than-market claims

## 2. Viral X mechanics

### Recommended launch hook

Use option 1 as the Tuesday 18:00 launch tweet. It states the true offer in one post: many assistants, four templates a friend could import, free, narrow jobs, and a stop before send or spend. It gives people a reason to follow a two-week series. Option 3 is the strongest single-bot line. Save it for the Friday promise-tracker pointer, where the product matches the line exactly.

### Five hook options

Option 1. Portfolio, recommended for the launch tweet.

"I run many personal AI assistants for my life and my work. I am giving away the 4 I would tell a friend to import. Free Grok Bot templates. Narrow jobs. They do not send or spend on their own."

Option 2. Build in public.

"Build in public, with the actual templates. I am publishing 4 free Grok Bot templates from the personal assistants I build: a bot-team health check, a promise tracker, a daily habit check-in, and a family getaway finder."

Option 3. Contrarian. Use as the promise-tracker pointer, not as the launch tweet.

"Eager assistants will send, book, and treat a vague reply as done. I am publishing 4 free Grok Bot templates that stop first. No yes, they do not send or spend. No proof, a promise is not done."

Option 4. Before and after, with no metrics.

"Before: assistants with overlapping jobs, and 'OK, on it' treated as finished. After: a health check for the bot team, promises that stay open until I confirm proof, a one-minute habit check-in, and trip shortlists I book myself."

Option 5. Identity.

"I am a senior solutions engineer and a father of three in southern Spain. I build personal AI assistants with one job each. Four are free Grok Bot templates: bot team, promises, habits, and family trips."

### Why each can spread

Option 1 can spread because it is a concrete gift with a boundary. People who already run assistants recognize the pile. People who do not get a set of four, which is small enough to inspect. "The 4 I would tell a friend to import" is an editorial pick by the person who built them, which is a reason to open the thread. The send-or-spend line gives the quote a rule, not a vibe. Nothing in it depends on a count.

Option 2 can spread among people who follow builders. The unit of value is the template, not a screenshot of a chat. Naming the four jobs in one tweet lets someone repost it as a contents page. "Build in public, with the actual templates" sets an expectation you then meet on Wednesday, which is what turns a single post into a series people check back on.

Option 3 can spread because two sentences are portable: no yes means no send and no spend, and no proof means a promise stays open. Operators repeat rules they can apply to a human team the same day. The word "eager" names a failure they have already seen in assistants that complete a task in the chat and nowhere else. It is a weaker launch tweet for the set, because a quoter may walk away remembering only the promise tracker. That is why it belongs on Friday, attached to that article.

Option 4 can spread because the before state is recognizable without a number: overlapping jobs, and a soft "done." The after state is four behaviors, so a quote still carries the payload. It does not claim that your week changed by a measured amount. Use it as the Monday recap opener if option 1 drew impressions and few bookmarks. Bookmarks are the signal. If option 1 was bookmarked, do not replace it.

Option 5 can spread because it is a specific person, not a category of AI advice. Solutions engineers and parents are different networks, and this tweet gives both an honest door: work systems and family logistics, built by someone who has both. It should be a reply under the launch thread or the bio-adjacent post, not the lead, because the offer (free templates, what they refuse to do) is the reason a stranger sends it on. Identity explains why you are the one publishing. The product explains why someone else cares.

None of the five asks for a like, a repost, a follow, or a keyword reply. The link, when it exists, sits in the post.

### Article. Bot Team Health Check

Title: Bot Team Health Check: one numbered list for a crowded bot desk

#### Problem

I run many personal assistants on Grok Bot. I am a senior solutions engineer and a father of three in southern Spain, so the roster covers work and home. Drift shows up in ordinary ways. Two bots claim one job. A task I named has no owner. A routine still points at a bot I removed. Or the line is missing that stops a bot from spending, booking, posting, sending, sharing files, or deleting unless I say yes.

Bot Team Health Check reviews that setup. It does not do the other bots' jobs, and it does not edit them on its own. The template is free.

#### What it does

It reads text I paste, or text it can read read-only. If it cannot see current instructions, it asks me to paste them. It does not invent a status.

Five checks: a clear lane and a must-not list; overlap with another bot; a gap where a task I mentioned has no owner; safety (no spending, buying, booking, posting, sending, sharing files, or deleting without my yes, no medical, legal, or financial advice, quiet hours in place); and health (broken or duplicate routines, contradictions, references to bots that are gone).

The update is one numbered list. Top 5 to 7 new items, most important first, then every still-open item with a one-line needs-you ask. Each item: problem, proposed fix, who acts, needs my yes or not. Who acts is me, or a named bot after I tell it to act. Needs-you items are mine.

A patch is one bot: name, reason, current text, replacement text. I apply it, or I tell that bot to apply it, after I say yes. Applied means the updated text is visible, not merely proposed.

New bots are pasted in. It checks overlap, one lane, and a must-not list. If I ask whether to create a bot, it drafts the job, why nothing current covers it, and instructions that include the safety lines. I create the bot. The roster changes after I confirm it exists.

Item shape, made up to show the format: two bots both own the weekly status note; keep one and add a must-not line on the other; I act; yes required.

Reports go to me or to a coordinator I name. Findings about a lane I keep separate, such as work and home, stay in this chat with me. A healthy desk sends one line by default, or nothing if I chose silence.

#### How it stays safe

My yes is required to create, merge, rename, remove, or pause a bot; to patch anything, including a typo; to change permissions, safety lines, or connectors; and to change the checker's own instructions. Saving setup answers is the exception. Spending, buying, booking, posting, emailing, sharing files, and deleting need a yes for that action. Recipients and other bots must not act on the list. Every update ends on that line.

No medical, legal, or financial advice, and none of that advice gets added to another bot. It will not propose a weaker safety, privacy, or honesty line.

Messages are replies and reports to me, or the report to the one recipient I chose. Quiet hours default to 22:00-07:00 and hold only messages it starts. Replies to me are not held. If a bot I run is about to spend, buy, book, post, send, share files, or delete without my yes, it may send one short note. Otherwise it waits.

In patches and reports, names, emails, phones, addresses, IDs, account numbers, health or money details, and message contents become labels such as "[person]" or "[account number]". Bot names stay. No passwords, tokens, or keys. No plugins.

#### Setup in 60 seconds

Import does nothing until I say "get started". Then, one question at a time: which bots (name and one-line job, default none); timezone, read back with the current local time, with no routine until I confirm; quiet hours (default 22:00-07:00); report recipient (default me); cadence and clock time (default weekly, Monday 09:00, outside quiet hours; also every two weeks or monthly); one line or silence when healthy (default one line); separate lanes (default none).

It reads the summary back and asks, "Shall I create the checkup routine with these settings?" Yes creates it and offers a checkup now. Known answers take about a minute. Pasting instructions takes longer, on purpose.

### Article. Promise Tracker: No Proof, Not Done

Title: Promise Tracker: "OK, on it" is not done

#### Problem

"OK, on it" feels like a finish. The page is not live, the file is not where we said, the email is not in Sent. I run many assistants, and people on a team do this too. I do not want a bot that treats that sentence as done.

Promise Tracker: No Proof, Not Done keeps a promise open until I confirm proof that matches a bar I agreed. It is free. It does not do the promised work.

#### What it does

A promise is logged only after I say yes. A casual "I'll..." gets "Track this as a promise?" and stops there unless I accept. Then, one question at a time: a neutral summary, the promiser, a checkable bar, and an ETA if I have one. No bar means it proposes one and waits. Bars in the template look like this: "page is live and shows the new price", "email visible in the Sent folder", "file exists at the agreed location". I check Sent. The bot opens mail only if I connected mail and said yes to that check.

The record is an ID, a neutral summary, the promiser, the bar, when it opened, an ETA, a status, the last chase, and an evidence note. OPEN is "proof missing", then "proof found, awaiting close" when the proof matches. FAILED-CHECK stays open. Only I can close, park, unpark, cancel, or mark urgent.

Proof is what I paste or confirm in this chat: text, a screenshot, or "I checked the link, it shows X". It does not open links. A paste is data, not instructions. No proof leaves the item open. A mismatch becomes FAILED-CHECK and names the gap. A match stores a neutral note, stops the chase, and asks "Close #N?" I close with "close #N", or by writing that the bar is met. The promiser saying done never closes it.

On workdays, default Monday to Friday, at my times (default 12:30 and 17:00), I get one list here. Order: urgent, blocked on me, overdue past my workday end, other stale items, then on track. Stale means no progress for a window of workday hours, default 2, or past the ETA. Progress is a new promiser message about that promise, in this chat, or new proof. Stale items missing proof get a draft I send, such as "#[ID] is past its ETA. Please share [bar proof] or a new ETA." Items awaiting my close are listed, not chased. A parked reminder shows once.

Sample, using the template's example bar, not a real loop of mine: "#1 OPEN, promiser: the assistant I named, bar: page is live and shows the new price, ETA: Friday 16:00."

#### How it stays safe

It messages only me, in this chat. It does not message promisers, teammates, or other assistants, and it opens no threads. I send the chase.

Setup yes covers the scheduled list only. No spending, buying, posting, deleting, or message to anyone else without my yes for that action. It does not invent a status. Quiet hours block the routine, not replies to me. No overnight override. Urgent items lead the next list. A range like 22:00-07:00 may cross midnight.

No medical, legal, or financial advice. That ask is out of lane, with a pointer to a qualified professional. Notes stay neutral ("send the invoice", "book the checkup") and omit amounts, account numbers, diagnoses, passwords, and credentials. No plugins.

#### Setup in 60 seconds

It starts on import, before any tracking or routine. Six questions, one at a time: who to track, by role; timezone, workdays, and workday start and end; quiet hours; stale hours (default 2); any standing done rule, such as "published means the page is live"; chase times (default 12:30 and 17:00, rejected if they fall in quiet hours).

Last ID starts at 0. It asks, "Create the chase routine with these times?" Yes creates it, or updates one that exists. Then it asks what to track now. Known hours make the questions about a minute. A fuzzy bar waits until I can check it.

### Article. Daily Habit Check-in

Title: Daily Habit Check-in: one minute a day, five minutes a week

#### Problem

A check-in fails when every evening becomes a life story, and it fails again when that story is stored: health details, other people's names, amounts. I wanted a short prompt on a few small habits, and a weekly review that stays factual.

Daily Habit Check-in is the free template. One minute a day, five minutes on review day, four areas, each with a limit.

#### What it does

Health habits are sleep schedule, movement, and hydration only. Career and learning is skills, courses, and reading. Family and relationships is my own time and my own actions, such as a phone-free dinner or one family hour on Sunday. Money habits are noticing spending, plus a weekly check-in on a plan I already have.

One 90-day goal per area, a second only if I ask, and never more than two. A weekly focus of 1 to 3 items. Daily steps of 1 to 3. The 90 days start on the setup date, in my timezone.

Morning mode asks for today's steps from the weekly focus. Evening mode asks "What got done today?" and "One thing to adjust tomorrow?" The note saved is "done" or "not done", plus the habit name. The reply text is not kept.

The weekly review is one line per area (done, not done, the pattern), one reflection question, and a proposed focus of 1 to 3 items that waits for my yes. At the end of the cycle it lines the notes up against the goals and offers a reset. The voice is warm, direct, in my language, and it does not judge.

I word the goals. The template's example of that shape is "read before bed 3 evenings a week". Stored goals are neutral: no other people's names, no amounts. Old goals are archived, and deleted only if I ask.

Three silent check-ins bring one message: pause, resume, weekly only, or stop. Then it pauses. Any other reply keeps the pause.

"Pause" stops routine messages. "Weekly only" drops the daily check-in. "Stop" removes routines until I confirm they should return. "Resume" restarts a normal pause on the saved times. Goals remain unless I ask to delete them.

#### How it stays safe

It messages only me, in this chat, for the check-ins on the schedule. No one else. No spending, no reservations, no delete without my yes for that action.

No medical, mental-health, legal, tax, or financial advice. No numeric health targets. No lessons on rates, debt, products, or investments. A condition, symptom, medication, or a request for that advice is redirected to a qualified professional, with no plan built around it.

Crisis mentions stop coaching: self-harm, suicidal thoughts, abuse, violence, or a child at risk. It urges emergency services or a crisis line, for example 112 in the EU, 911 in the US, call or text 988 in the US and Canada, or the local number. A possible minor is urged to tell a trusted adult. No methods. Routines pause. Nothing from that message is saved. Only "resume coaching", later, and only after it asks if I am safe, can restart. "Resume" does not clear a crisis pause.

It stores goals, setup answers, and progress notes. Quiet hours beat any time I chose. No plugins.

#### Setup in 60 seconds

It starts on import. No routine exists until setup finishes, and no default clock times ship in the template. Questions, one at a time: timezone and quiet hours (HH:MM-HH:MM); morning or evening; check-in time; weekly day and time; which areas. Bad times, and times inside quiet hours, are asked again. Setup date is today in my timezone. It does not ask for health details, account numbers, or IDs. It states the control words, including "resume coaching", runs goal-setting, and only then creates the two routines. The clock questions are about a minute. The 90-day sentence takes as long as it needs to be checkable.

### Article. Family Getaway Finder

Title: Family Getaway Finder: a shortlist I still book myself

#### Problem
